Projects

Optimizing Vehicle Acquisition Pricing

Business Problem:

Automotive dealerships must balance two competing goals: acquiring enough vehicles to support inventory needs while also protecting front-end and wholesale profitability. Offering too little may reduce acquisition volume, while offering too much can increase cost and reduce margins.

Project Goal:

The goal of this project was to explore how appraisal offer adjustments impact acquisition probability and profitability. The project evaluates whether data science can help identify pricing thresholds where additional offer increases produce diminishing returns.

Data Used:

The analysis used vehicle appraisal and acquisition data, including appraisal values, wholesale values, mileage, vehicle characteristics, acquisition outcomes, and profitability measures.

Methods:

  • Exploratory data analysis
  • Feature engineering
  • Logistic regression
  • Decision tree models
  • Gradient boosting models
  • Tradeoff curve analysis

Key Insight:

The project found that increasing offer values may improve acquisition probability, but the benefit is not unlimited. At some point, additional offer increases may create smaller gains in acquisition while placing pressure on retail and wholesale margins.

Business Value:

This type of model could support appraisal teams by recommending more disciplined offer adjustments. Instead of increasing offers broadly, the business could identify where a higher offer is most likely to improve acquisition outcomes while still protecting profitability.
